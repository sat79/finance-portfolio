# Kaspi: an established ecosystem, with Türkiye as a test of repeatability
Research update: 13 September 2026. Supplements the existing dividend valuation; its numerical assumptions and valuation outputs have not been increased in response to this narrative.

## Investment thesis
Kaspi's attractive feature is the interaction between commerce, payments and financial services. A useful investment thesis asks whether that interaction supports durable, distributable earnings, and whether the capability can be transferred to a new market at an acceptable return on capital.

The Kazakhstan business provides evidence of scale and profitability. Türkiye provides a larger potential runway, but also integration, funding, currency and underwriting demands. These belong in separate analytical layers: proven operating performance, management's expansion plan, and the investor's assumptions about its eventual value.

## Reviewing the supplied interviews
The Fintech Leaders article is Miguel Armaza's 11 August 2026 interview with Mikhail Lomtadze. Its Türkiye discussion explicitly describes an intention to apply Kaspi's super-app approach, initially improving the existing customer experience. The Couchonomics episode is hosted by Arjun Singh; the page describes shopping, payments and government services. Neither accessible page attributes the argument to Parameswaran. The supplied Spotify link resolves to a Fintech Leaders episode title, but its audio/transcript was not accessible here. These are management-interview sources, not three independent confirmations of investment value. [1–3]

“Ultimate super app” is an opinion, not a measurable valuation input. The comparison with single-purpose Western apps is a useful shorthand, but too absolute for an investment memo. The relevant question is how effectively services share customer relationships, distribution and transaction infrastructure.

## Engagement: use the actual measure
The FY2025 report gives Kazakhstan average MAU of 15.7 million and average DAU of 10.7 million, a 68% ratio. This is substantial engagement, but does not establish that almost the entire country uses Kaspi daily. Different-country customer counts, monthly users and daily users are not interchangeable. [4, PDF p51]

Calculated: core revenue growth 18.7%, core net-income growth 9.8%; the Turkish loss absorbs 8.0% of core income, leaving group net-income growth of 1.0%. “Kazakhstan & Other” includes Azerbaijan and Ukraine. Net income is group income, not attributable earnings. Acquisition scope and Türkiye's IAS 29 accounting require care. [4]

This supports a profitable-core thesis, but not a claim that expansion has already reproduced those economics. Reported revenue growth can also reflect consolidation, product mix, inflation or exchange rates. It is not automatically organic growth or cash available to shareholders.

## Latest profitability check
Q2 2026 revenue rose 15%, while net income was broadly flat at KZT258.889bn. Reported net margin was 23.3%, versus 26.7% a year earlier. Profitability remains substantial, but profit conversion has weakened. [5, PDF p14]

## Türkiye: strategy already in execution
Hepsiburada supplies the commerce entry point. Kaspi reports completion of the Rabobank Türkiye acquisition in July, rebranding as Hepsi Bank, an integrated shopping-loan pilot, broader fintech expansion planned from 2027, and approximately $300m initial bank capitalisation intended. These are concrete steps and stated plans, not proof of returns. The release says the initial loan uses Hepsiburada's existing microfinance licence; do not imply the full banking rollout is already complete. [5, PDF pp2,6]

## How the ecosystem could create value — analyst interpretation
1. **Useful daily tasks create repeat visits.** Payments and other frequent activities may make distribution of additional services cheaper. Verify this through retention and acquisition cost; the app's breadth alone proves neither.
2. **Merchants add utility.** More relevant sellers and easier checkout can increase purchase frequency and conversion. Growth bought with subsidies is less valuable than growth with improving contribution profit.
3. **Financial services can support commerce.** Embedded lending may lift conversion and repeat buying. The benefit must exceed funding, expected credit losses, servicing costs and capital charges.
4. **Transaction history may improve decisions.** Better information could support fraud control, personalisation and underwriting. A claim of better lending outcomes requires performance by origination vintage, not a general claim about “more data”.
5. **Convenience can support retention.** It does not eliminate competition, regulatory constraints or a customer's ability to use several apps.

Government services can contribute usefulness and trust without necessarily producing meaningful direct revenue. Assigning them a separate valuation would require evidence of incremental cash flows.

## Türkiye milestone scorecard
This is a proposed diligence framework, not management guidance or a completed performance dashboard.

| Hypothesis | Evidence to monitor | What would weaken the thesis? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Existing customers adopt added services | Eligible-customer conversion, repeat use and cross-product retention | Promotions lift first use but not retention |
| Commerce scales profitably | Orders, delivery reliability, contribution profit after incentives | Growth depends on discounts, shipping subsidy or rising acquisition cost |
| Lending improves economics | Vintage arrears, net credit losses, funding spread and capital-adjusted return | Volume rises faster than mature-vintage evidence or losses consume the spread |
| The business can fund its own expansion | Cash needs, capital injections, cash upstreamed and minority interests | Repeated injections postpone distributions without measurable returns |
| Local advantages develop | Merchant adoption, customer satisfaction and sustainable pricing | Kazakhstan's distribution advantages do not transfer to local competition |
| Reported growth survives currency effects | TRY operating results, inflation-adjusted comparisons and USD/KZT translation | Nominal growth obscures weak real returns or currency losses |

## Implications for the valuation
Keep the existing dividend scenarios as conditional illustrations. This update does not justify adding an arbitrary “Türkiye premium” to the $73.11 base output or assuming a second Kazakhstan in the terminal value.

A stronger next model would forecast Kazakhstan & Other and Türkiye separately, then bridge their cash needs to parent distributions. Model acquisition funding, the incremental bank capital requirement, ownership/minority interests, currency translation and country-specific required returns. Regulatory capital invested in a bank is an equity funding requirement, not an operating expense to deduct twice. Equity cash-flow valuation for the lending business and an appropriate commerce valuation should reconcile to one group equity value with intercompany eliminations.

The existing dividend assumptions may already implicitly reflect growth or retention for Türkiye. Adding a separate Turkish value without removing that overlap would double count. Likewise, consolidated group income is not automatically cash available to the ADS holder.

**Bear interpretation:** expansion consumes capital, credit costs disappoint and payout is delayed. **Base interpretation:** the established business funds a measured rollout; upside requires evidence of improving incremental returns. **Bull interpretation:** repeat use and profitable cross-selling develop in Türkiye, with cash generation exceeding reinvestment needs. These are qualitative scenarios; probabilities, terminal margins and values have not been fabricated.

## What this adds to the portfolio
The project now connects competitive advantage to financial evidence and capital allocation. The interview discussion becomes: what makes the core valuable, what is being built in Türkiye, what evidence would demonstrate success, and how those judgments should enter a valuation. That is stronger than treating the super-app label as a reason to pay more.
